1. Who we are

Signal-23 is an independent electronic music duo, Glenn Harless and Evan Simonsen. We operate this website and an internal Meta developer app that we use only to manage our own advertising on Facebook and Instagram. You can reach us at music@signal23.net.

2. Visiting the website

Measurement. Most of this site uses no analytics, advertising pixels or tracking cookies, and sets no cookies of its own. We do not build profiles of visitors. The exception is our listen pages, at addresses starting signal23.net/listen/, where our ads send people. What happens there is described under our advertising.

Browser storage. The terminal page stores a single value in your browser's session storage so it does not replay its introduction during the same visit; it disappears when you close the tab or browser. If you make a Meta measurement choice on a listen page, it is kept in your browser's local storage so the page remembers it. Neither value is sent to us.

Hosting. The site is hosted by Netlify. To deliver pages and protect the service, Netlify processes your IP address and standard request details such as the page requested, your browser type and the time of the request, under Netlify's privacy policy.

Fonts. Our typeface loads from Google Fonts. When it loads, Google receives your IP address and browser details, under Google's privacy policy.

Audio and video. The terminal's SCAN stream is served from Dropbox, so playing it sends your IP address to Dropbox. Films and excerpts on the A/V page play from YouTube, and only once you press play; pressing play sends your IP address and browser details to YouTube, which may also set cookies, under Google's privacy policy. Other audio, video and images, including the film stills, are served from our own site.

Links to other services. We link to Spotify, Bandcamp, SoundCloud, YouTube, Instagram and DistroKid, and some of our advertising sends people directly to Spotify. Once you follow a link, that service's own privacy policy applies.

Email. If you write to us, we receive your email address and message and use them to reply. There is no mailing-list sign-up on this site.

3. Instrument racks: downloads and payments

Free downloads. If you choose to pay nothing, your browser asks our server for a temporary download link that expires after 30 minutes. We do not ask for your name or email address, and we store nothing about you. The file is delivered from Cloudflare R2 storage, which receives your IP address when you download it.

Paid downloads. Payments are handled by Stripe on Stripe's own checkout pages. Stripe collects your card details, email address and billing information under Stripe's privacy policy. We never see your full card number.

After you pay, our server asks Stripe to confirm the payment and receives the pack you bought, the amount, and the email address you gave Stripe, so the confirmation page can show your receipt address and give you the download link. Stripe also notifies our server when a checkout completes. That notification, including the email address and amount, is written to our hosting provider's function logs, which Netlify keeps for a limited period that Netlify sets.

We do not keep a customer database. Your payment record lives in our Stripe account, where we keep it for as long as we need it for accounting, tax and dispute purposes.

4. Our advertising on Facebook and Instagram

We advertise our own music on Facebook and Instagram. To manage that advertising we registered a developer app with Meta. The app is an internal tool used only by the members of Signal-23. It is not offered to the public, nobody else can connect their accounts to it, and it does not provide any login or feature on this website.

What the app accesses. Through Meta's Marketing API, authorized operators can view and manage:

  • identifiers for our own Meta Business, Facebook Page, Instagram account and ad accounts;
  • campaign, ad set and ad settings, including budgets, schedules, placements and audience settings;
  • the creative assets we upload: video, images and ad text;
  • advertising reports such as spend, impressions, reach, clicks and audience breakdowns.

Meta provides those reports in aggregated form. We do not receive the names, profile details or contact information of people who see or interact with our ads, and we do not try to identify them.

What we do not do with it. We do not use the app to read or send direct messages, moderate or reply to comments, publish ordinary posts or stories, or manage anyone else's Pages, accounts or ad accounts. Some permissions granted to the app cover broader capabilities than this; we use them only for the advertising tasks described here.

Listen pages. Some of our ads send people to a page on this site for the advertised track, at an address starting signal23.net/listen/, with a button that opens the track on Spotify. Two kinds of measurement run there, and nowhere else on the site.

Our own visit counter, for everyone. When a listen page loads, and when you tap its Spotify button, your browser sends our server a short note: which page, which of our ads you came from (the utm_content label in the ad's link), whether it was a visit or a tap, and a device category (phone, computer, or the Instagram or Facebook in-app browser) worked out from your browser's user-agent string. We keep only counts of those notes per day. The counter uses no cookies, and we do not store your IP address, your full browser details or the time of your visit, so the counts cannot be linked back to you. It runs whatever you choose below.

Meta Pixel. Listen pages can also use the Meta Pixel, Meta's measurement script. Whether it runs depends on where you are, which our hosting provider works out from your IP address when the page loads:

  • In the EU or EEA, the UK or Switzerland, or if we can't tell where you are: the page asks first. Nothing is loaded from Meta, and nothing is sent to Meta, unless you tap Accept.
  • Everywhere else, including the United States: it runs when the page loads, unless you have turned it off with the Cookies link or your browser sends a Global Privacy Control signal.

When it runs, the Pixel sets Meta cookies on this site (_fbp, and _fbc if you arrived from one of our ads) and tells Meta that you viewed the page and, if you do, that you tapped the Spotify button, along with the track, the campaign labels in the ad's link, the page address, your IP address and browser details. When you tap the button, our server also sends Meta that same tap, with the same details and Meta's cookies, through Meta's Conversions API, so Meta still receives it if your browser's own request is blocked or cut off as Spotify opens; Meta counts the two copies once. Meta uses this to report how our ads perform and to show them to people likely to listen, and may connect it with your Facebook or Instagram account, under Meta's privacy policy. We receive only Meta's aggregated reports.

Your choice. The Cookies link at the bottom of any listen page turns Meta measurement on or off for that browser; the Spotify button works the same either way. If you decline, or your browser sends a Global Privacy Control signal, the Pixel does not load and nothing goes to Meta. We treat Global Privacy Control as a request to opt out of sharing for targeted advertising wherever you are. If you turn measurement off after it ran, we stop sending Meta events and remove the Meta cookies we can reach. What was already sent stays with Meta; you can review and disconnect it in your Facebook or Instagram settings under "Your activity off Meta technologies".

5. AI tools we use

Authorized operators use AI assistant tools to help draft ads, prepare creative, and review our account settings and performance reports. When they do, the advertising data described above, such as account identifiers, ad settings and aggregated reports, may be sent to the AI provider to produce the response. The providers we use are Anthropic (Claude) and OpenAI (Codex); each processes that data under its own privacy policy and terms. The data involved is about our own accounts and aggregated audiences, not about individual visitors to this site or individual buyers.

6. How access is restricted

Our Meta app and ad accounts, Stripe account, Cloudflare storage and Netlify hosting can be used only by the members of Signal-23, each with their own login. The keys and tokens these services use are stored as private settings on our hosting provider and are not part of the website's public code.

We do not sell personal information. We share information only with the service providers named in this policy, as needed to run the site, take payments, deliver downloads and run our advertising, or when the law requires it.

7. How long we keep information

  • Website visits: we keep no records of individual visitors. Listen page counts are kept per day for as long as they help us judge our advertising. The terminal's browser flag ends with your session; a listen page's record of your Meta answer stays in your browser until you change it or clear this site's data. Hosting and function logs are kept by Netlify for the limited period Netlify sets. If Meta measurement ran during your visit, Meta keeps what it received under its own retention.
  • Downloads and payments: download links expire after 30 minutes. Payment records stay in our Stripe account while we need them for accounting, tax and dispute purposes. The email address in a completed-checkout notification stays in Netlify's function logs for that same limited period.
  • Advertising data: account identifiers, ad settings, creative and reports live in our Meta ad account under Meta's retention. Copies we make, such as exported reports, creative files, notes and AI tool conversation history, stay in the operators' private working files and tool accounts while they are useful for running our advertising, and are deleted when they are no longer needed.
  • Email: we keep correspondence for as long as we need it to reply and handle any follow-up.

8. Contact and deletion requests

To ask what information we hold about you, or to ask us to delete it, email music@signal23.net from the address you used with us and tell us what you would like removed. We will delete the information we hold, or ask the relevant provider to delete it, except for records we must keep for payment, tax or legal reasons, and we will reply to confirm.

Because Meta gives us advertising reports only in aggregate, we cannot identify or remove an individual from them, and our own listen page counts hold nothing that identifies you. Requests about your own Facebook or Instagram data go to Meta through your account settings. Requests about a payment can also be made to Stripe directly.

9. Changes to this policy

When our practices change we will update this page and the effective date at the top. The current version is always at signal23.net/legal/privacy.
